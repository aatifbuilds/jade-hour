
## The realistic drink: scroll-scrubbed video (6-7 Nov 2026)
**Why:** the 3D drink looked "like a kid drew it" next to the reference reel, whose drink is an AI-generated video. Live 3D cannot reach that, so the page now scrubs a video made in Google Flow (Veo) by the owner. The 3D drink remains as an automatic fallback (no frames, or load failure).
**The footage:** six 8-second clips from one starting glass image (a ribbed café glass): ice, milk, matcha, stir, foam, yuzu. They are not chained by the tool, so ffmpeg stitches them with 0.25-0.5 s dissolves where the glass states match (clips 4 to 6 each start from the previous clip's last frame, cleaned). 32.5 s total, cut to 320 WebP frames (desktop 6.8 MB, phone 4 MB), cropped to 4:5 with a small zoom that trims the top strip (fingertips appeared there in the stir clip).
**Fixes along the way:** the Veo sparkle watermark in a start frame was copied into the next clip as drifting sparkles, so the foam clip was cleaned with a temporal median filter and the next start frame had the watermark and specks removed; the yuzu clip, once started from the cleaned frame, needed no filtering.
**How it works:** `public/frames/story.json` says which chapters the video tells and where each begins on the page and in the video; the page builds its text from it, and a smooth monotone map (`src/scroll/beats.ts`) turns scroll into video position, with a critically damped spring for glide and a canvas player that loads the frames nearest the visitor first and cross-fades between frames.
**Honest limits:** the source is 720p, so the picture is a little soft once cropped and enlarged; real frame rate on a phone is untested (my browser caps at 30 fps); a few faint specks remain in the foam clip.

## Full-bleed stage and the scroll zoom (7 Nov 2026)
**Why:** the owner compared our page with the reel: (1) the reel zooms in and out as you scroll, ours did not; (2) the reel's glass stands alone in the page, ours sat in a bordered panel whose backdrop colour (`#c9c4bc`) differed from the page's (`#ecebe7`), which read as a cutout.
**What changed:**
- **Frames cut to the safe region** (x 12-88%, y 6%-bottom of the 1280x720 source): this removes Veo's watermark (bottom-right) and the fingertips at the top edge for good, so a full-screen picture can never show them. Desktop = the whole safe region (974x678, native size, light sharpen); phone = a centred 3:4 box (508x678). 260 frames: 3.3 MB desktop, 2.8 MB phone.
- **A camera** (`src/scroll/camera.ts`): `cameraRect` picks which part of the frame to show for a zoom and focus point and never shows outside the frame; `storyView` moves the camera smoothly through a view per chapter, stored in `public/frames/story.json` (wide for the hero, in for the ice and pours, in on the top of the glass for foam and yuzu, out for the finish). Magnification is capped (2.6 screen pixels per picture pixel) so a big screen zooms a little less rather than going soft.
- **Layout:** one fixed full-screen canvas; the page takes the video's backdrop colour (on a phone, the colour of the picture's bottom edge, which the text area continues); hero name stacked on the left, then a vertical list of the steps (named, not numbered; the current one in full ink; click to jump and the spring glides); the step's words in one fixed layer on the right that fades and rises in. Phone: picture across the top 62% of the screen, words below.
**Honest limits:** the source is 720p, so the zoom is soft on a large screen (1920 wide: about 2.6x); real phone speed untested (my browser caps at 30 fps); the muted step colour is 3.5:1, which passes only for large type.

## The opening shot pulled back (7 Nov 2026)
**Owner:** the original glass position is too zoomed in. At zoom 1 the glass filled about 85% of the screen height, because 1 = the whole (cropped) frame and the glass is most of that frame.
**Fix:** the camera can now pull back past the edge of the frame (`cameraPlacement`, zoom down to 0.5). The hero is at 0.7 (glass about 60% of the height), the ice and milk steps sit near 1, the pours and stir slightly in, foam and yuzu closest (1.35, focused on the top of the glass), and the finish pulls back to 0.85. When the frame no longer covers the screen, the margin is painted from the frame's four measured corner colours (the backdrop is a real lighting gradient, light on the left and dark at the top right, so no single colour fits), and the frame's edges are baked soft (alpha fade, 12% of the shorter side) so they melt into it.
**Bugs found on the way:** (1) sharp's `stats()` ignores `extract()`, so the "backdrop colour" had been the average of the whole frame; the page colour and the phone text-area colour are now sampled properly. (2) The player kept every decoded frame in memory (about 690 MB on desktop): it now keeps a window of about 97 frames around the visitor (`framesToEvict`, `nextToLoad` with a radius), which also makes a phone crash much less likely.
**Also:** nav text darkened (`--ink-deep`) because it can land on the darkest corner of the backdrop (4.4:1 became 5.4:1); on a phone the glass never goes smaller than zoom 0.9. 108 tests.

## Apple-style scroll and button behaviour, ported from RILL (7 Nov 2026)
**Owner:** bring over the scroll/click changes from the earlier Apple-inspired project.
**What RILL did, and what jade hour has now:**
1. A critically damped spring smooths the scroll (fast flicks glide, nothing overshoots): already present; its speed is now `OMEGA.normal` (10), about 0.5 s to settle a full-length jump.
2. **Buttons jump the scroll instantly and the picture glides there** on a slower spring (`OMEGA.glide` 4.2, about 1.6 s), then eases back to normal speed over 0.5 s (`springOmega`). No CSS smooth scroll (it would fly through the recipe). New shared handler `src/ui/anchors.ts` for every `#step` link: the top links (The drink, Steps, Letter, Order) and the step list.
3. **Any wheel, touch or key press hands control straight back** (the glide stops at once).
4. **Step hysteresis** (`chapterWithHysteresis`, margin 0.008 of the scroll): the step cannot flicker at a boundary; a click switches at once and shows only the destination, never the steps the picture glides past.
**Measured in the browser (dev hook):** click Ice to Final pour: lands exactly on the target, overshoot about 0, 50% in 0.39 s, 95% in 1.1 s, 99% in 1.6 s, destination text shown immediately; the same jump as a plain scroll: 95% in 0.48 s, no overshoot (the glide is about 2.4x calmer); a wheel during a glide stops it at once; a top link glides home; jitter of plus or minus 30 px across a boundary: 0 step changes; a steady scroll changes the step exactly once per step in order, forwards (7) and back (7). Same in Chromium and Safari's engine; the phone's Order button glides.
**Difference from RILL:** RILL's glide moved a 3D pose straight from scene to scene; here the picture is a video, so a long jump slowly scrubs through the frames in between (a calm time-lapse) while the text shows the destination. A dissolve-jump could replace it if that feels odd. 122 tests.
**Not provable from here:** the feel on a real trackpad or phone swipe; `/review-animations` is for the owner to run.

## A button click goes straight to the step (8 Nov 2026, from the live site)
**Owner:** clicking from Steps to Letter "loads all the scrolls and seems very off; this was fixed with RILL".
**Cause:** a click jumped the scroll, but the smoothed progress then swept the whole distance, so the picture scrubbed through every frame in between (197 frame requests in one click, stale flashes while they arrived) and the camera zoomed through every step's view on the way (1.0, 1.3, 1.4...). RILL never showed the scenes in between: its camera went straight from the old pose to the new one.
**Fix:** for a jump of more than one step (progress distance over 0.2): the picture on screen is frozen as a snapshot, the video jumps to the destination, and the new picture fades in over the snapshot (0.8 s) once its frames are decoded (never waiting more than 1.2 s); the camera blends straight from the old view to the destination's view (`mixView`, a spring at the glide speed). A hop of one step or less still scrubs slowly. A wheel, touch or key ends the dissolve at once. The load window is now 30 frames either side (it was 48).
**Measured (dev hook + request counts), Steps to Letter:** frame requests 197 to 61 (only the destination's neighbourhood); zoom 1.01 to 0.90 smoothly with no wobble; the old picture is held at 60 ms and the new one has taken over by about 400 ms; one-step hop still scrubs; a wheel during a dissolve hands control back; a second click mid-glide ends on the second target; The drink returns exactly to the opening view (0.7); same in Safari's engine; the phone's Order button works. 129 tests.

## Smooth on a phone: the network out of the scroll path (8 Nov 2026, from the owner's phone recording)
**Owner:** a screen recording of the live site on their phone: the glass animation looks "a little janky". **What the recording showed** (frame analysis, 613 frames): a clean 60 fps recording, but during motion 39 frames where the picture barely changed (often in pairs) followed by 11 catch-up jumps, and big/small alternation in the per-frame change: a picture that freezes, then jumps.
**Cause (measured on a simulated phone: 4x slower CPU, 4 Mbps, 90 ms):** decoding a frame about 2 ms, drawing about 0 ms, no long tasks, but **fetching a frame about 96 ms**. The loader fetched only a window of frames, 4 at a time, so it could bring in about 40 a second; a hard fling needs more, so the picture used the nearest older frame and then jumped.
**Fix:** loading is now two steps. FETCH (background, network): every frame is fetched once right after load, nearest the visitor first, 10 at a time, and its compressed bytes are kept (3 to 4 MB in all). DECODE (local, about 2 ms): the window of 30 frames either side is decoded from memory (`nextToDecode`), so scrolling never waits for the network; decoded pictures are still only kept for the window, so memory is unchanged. The adaptive resolution (which resized and cleared the canvas, a visible pop) now runs for the heavy 3D fallback only.
**Measured, same three hard flings, cache off, simulated phone:** stale pictures (older frame shown) about 10% before, **0% after** (1.5 s, 4 s and 9 s after load); worst case (fling starts 0.1 s after load on 4 Mbps) 0.9%; on about 20 Mbps 0%. A button click now makes 0 new frame requests. Decoded frames 61 (unchanged), clicks, dissolve and hand-back unchanged, Chromium and Safari's engine, 0 console errors. 133 tests.
**Honest limits:** a simulated phone is a proxy (not an iPhone's GPU or the owner's network); the dev counters (`__story.stats`) are stripped from production; if some unevenness remains the next suspects are the camera's zoom speed through chapter transitions and iOS scroll-event timing.
