
## The realistic drink: scroll-scrubbed video (6-7 Nov 2026)
**Why:** the 3D drink looked "like a kid drew it" next to the reference reel, whose drink is an AI-generated video. Live 3D cannot reach that, so the page now scrubs a video made in Google Flow (Veo) by the owner. The 3D drink remains as an automatic fallback (no frames, or load failure).
**The footage:** six 8-second clips from one starting glass image (a ribbed café glass): ice, milk, matcha, stir, foam, yuzu. They are not chained by the tool, so ffmpeg stitches them with 0.25-0.5 s dissolves where the glass states match (clips 4 to 6 each start from the previous clip's last frame, cleaned). 32.5 s total, cut to 320 WebP frames (desktop 6.8 MB, phone 4 MB), cropped to 4:5 with a small zoom that trims the top strip (fingertips appeared there in the stir clip).
**Fixes along the way:** the Veo sparkle watermark in a start frame was copied into the next clip as drifting sparkles, so the foam clip was cleaned with a temporal median filter and the next start frame had the watermark and specks removed; the yuzu clip, once started from the cleaned frame, needed no filtering.
**How it works:** `public/frames/story.json` says which chapters the video tells and where each begins on the page and in the video; the page builds its text from it, and a smooth monotone map (`src/scroll/beats.ts`) turns scroll into video position, with a critically damped spring for glide and a canvas player that loads the frames nearest the visitor first and cross-fades between frames.
**Honest limits:** the source is 720p, so the picture is a little soft once cropped and enlarged; real frame rate on a phone is untested (my browser caps at 30 fps); a few faint specks remain in the foam clip.

## Full-bleed stage and the scroll zoom (7 Nov 2026)
**Why:** the owner compared our page with the reel: (1) the reel zooms in and out as you scroll, ours did not; (2) the reel's glass stands alone in the page, ours sat in a bordered panel whose backdrop colour (`#c9c4bc`) differed from the page's (`#ecebe7`), which read as a cutout.
**What changed:**
- **Frames cut to the safe region** (x 12-88%, y 6%-bottom of the 1280x720 source): this removes Veo's watermark (bottom-right) and the fingertips at the top edge for good, so a full-screen picture can never show them. Desktop = the whole safe region (974x678, native size, light sharpen); phone = a centred 3:4 box (508x678). 260 frames: 3.3 MB desktop, 2.8 MB phone.
- **A camera** (`src/scroll/camera.ts`): `cameraRect` picks which part of the frame to show for a zoom and focus point and never shows outside the frame; `storyView` moves the camera smoothly through a view per chapter, stored in `public/frames/story.json` (wide for the hero, in for the ice and pours, in on the top of the glass for foam and yuzu, out for the finish). Magnification is capped (2.6 screen pixels per picture pixel) so a big screen zooms a little less rather than going soft.
- **Layout:** one fixed full-screen canvas; the page takes the video's backdrop colour (on a phone, the colour of the picture's bottom edge, which the text area continues); hero name stacked on the left, then a vertical list of the steps (named, not numbered; the current one in full ink; click to jump and the spring glides); the step's words in one fixed layer on the right that fades and rises in. Phone: picture across the top 62% of the screen, words below.
**Honest limits:** the source is 720p, so the zoom is soft on a large screen (1920 wide: about 2.6x); real phone speed untested (my browser caps at 30 fps); the muted step colour is 3.5:1, which passes only for large type.

## The opening shot pulled back (7 Nov 2026)
**Owner:** the original glass position is too zoomed in. At zoom 1 the glass filled about 85% of the screen height, because 1 = the whole (cropped) frame and the glass is most of that frame.
**Fix:** the camera can now pull back past the edge of the frame (`cameraPlacement`, zoom down to 0.5). The hero is at 0.7 (glass about 60% of the height), the ice and milk steps sit near 1, the pours and stir slightly in, foam and yuzu closest (1.35, focused on the top of the glass), and the finish pulls back to 0.85. When the frame no longer covers the screen, the margin is painted from the frame's four measured corner colours (the backdrop is a real lighting gradient, light on the left and dark at the top right, so no single colour fits), and the frame's edges are baked soft (alpha fade, 12% of the shorter side) so they melt into it.
**Bugs found on the way:** (1) sharp's `stats()` ignores `extract()`, so the "backdrop colour" had been the average of the whole frame; the page colour and the phone text-area colour are now sampled properly. (2) The player kept every decoded frame in memory (about 690 MB on desktop): it now keeps a window of about 97 frames around the visitor (`framesToEvict`, `nextToLoad` with a radius), which also makes a phone crash much less likely.
**Also:** nav text darkened (`--ink-deep`) because it can land on the darkest corner of the backdrop (4.4:1 became 5.4:1); on a phone the glass never goes smaller than zoom 0.9. 108 tests.
